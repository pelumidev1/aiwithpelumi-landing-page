
# Prompt Pipeline — Ideal Reader Profiles

A working reference for the landing page and every content decision after it. Three reader segments in priority order. Each one is broken down all the way — demographics, job, tools, money, what keeps them up, where they hang out, what makes them subscribe, and what makes them bounce.

The through-line across all three: people who work in marketing or growth and want to *build* with AI, not just prompt it. Not engineers. Not AI tourists. The person in between.

---

## Segment 1 — The Marketer Who Wants to Build (primary, ~55% of target audience)

The core reader. Everything on the landing page speaks to this person first.

### Demographics

| Field              | Detail                                                                                   |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Age                | 27–38                                                                                   |
| Role               | Product marketer, growth marketer, content lead, marketing manager, PMM                  |
| Seniority          | 3–10 years in                                                                           |
| Income             | $70k–$140k (US/UK); ₦ equivalents and $ contract rates for African/remote              |
| Education          | Degree-holding, often non-technical (business, comms, liberal arts)                      |
| Location           | Remote-first. US, UK, Nigeria, Kenya, India, Philippines, EU                             |
| Company            | Startups and scale-ups, 10–500 people. B2B SaaS, fintech, crypto, DTC                   |
| Tools they live in | Notion, HubSpot/Marketo, Webflow, Zapier, Google Workspace, Claude/ChatGPT, Figma        |
| Technical level    | No-code power user hitting the ceiling. Can read code, can't confidently write it — yet |

### Psychographics

Ambitious and a little impatient. They see AI eating the boring 60% of their job and they'd rather ride that than be replaced by it. They respect people who ship. They're allergic to hype and to "thought leaders" who've never built anything. They want leverage, not another certificate.

### What they're stuck on

They can spec a campaign but hand the technical build to someone else — and it dies in a backlog. Every tool wants a subscription and still won't do the one specific thing they need. They've watched a dev wire up something in an afternoon that they'd have paid $200/month for. That stings, and it motivates.

### What they want

To write the positioning doc *and* ship the workflow that runs it. To stop being "just marketing." A real, defensible edge — the rare marketer who can build the system they designed.

### Where they already are

LinkedIn (heavy), X/Twitter marketing and build-in-public circles, r/marketing and r/SaaS, Lenny's Newsletter, Demand Curve, indie growth Discords, YouTube tutorials at 1.5x.

### Subscribe triggers

A concrete workflow they could copy today. Proof you actually built the thing. A post that names the exact frustration they had this week.

### Objections to beat

"Is this going to be too technical for me?" — No, that's the whole point. "Another AI newsletter?" — No, this one shows the build.

### Landing page lines

"You know marketing. Now build the systems that run it." / "Stop paying for a tool that almost does what you need. Build the one that does."

## Segment 2 — The Solo Operator / Technical Founder (~30%)

### Demographics

| Field              | Detail                                                                         |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Age                | 25–40                                                                         |
| Role               | Indie hacker, solo founder, technical co-founder, one-person growth team       |
| Seniority          | Runs the whole thing; growth landed on them by default                         |
| Income             | Highly variable — $0 pre-revenue to $200k+; often measured in MRR, not salary |
| Education          | Self-taught or CS-adjacent; many dropped the corporate track on purpose        |
| Location           | Global, remote. Strong in US, EU, India, Nigeria, SEA                          |
| Company            | 1–5 people. Their own product                                                 |
| Tools they live in | VS Code, GitHub, Vercel, Supabase, Stripe, the Claude/OpenAI APIs, Cursor      |
| Technical level    | Can code. Ships. The gap is marketing systems, not engineering                 |

### Psychographics

Autonomous, output-obsessed, skeptical of anything that smells like an agency deck. They read code faster than they read marketing copy. They'll respect you the moment you show a real repo or a real pipeline. They hate fluff more than the marketer does.

### What they're stuck on

Growth is a tax on their time. They *can* build the automation, but it's glue-work they keep putting off, so acquisition stays manual and slow. They don't want a marketing course. They want the plumbing diagram.

### What they want

Marketing that runs itself so they can get back to the product. Acquisition and conversion systems they can wire up once and forget.

### Where they already are

X/Twitter build-in-public, Indie Hackers, Hacker News, r/SaaS, r/EntrepreneurRideAlong, Y Combinator library, dev-heavy Discords and Slacks.

### Subscribe triggers

Code they can fork. A pipeline diagram. Honest "here's what broke" writeups. Zero patience for theory.

### Objections to beat

"I can figure this out myself." — Sure, but here's the version that already works. "Is this just marketing content?" — It's the plumbing, with the wiring shown.

### Landing page lines

"Marketing that runs while you build the product." / "The automation a marketer would outsource and an engineer won't bother with. I show the wiring."

## Segment 3 — The AI-Curious Professional Leveling Up (~15%, top of funnel)

The widest, coolest audience. Not marketers or builders yet — deciding whether AI is worth getting serious about. Your first post (the free Anthropic course) is aimed straight here. Some convert up into Segment 1 or 2 over months.

### Demographics

| Field              | Detail                                                                       |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Age                | 24–45                                                                       |
| Role               | Analysts, ops, consultants, PMs, freelancers, early-career knowledge workers |
| Seniority          | Mixed — junior to mid, plus curious senior people                           |
| Income             | $40k–$120k                                                                  |
| Education          | Degree-holding, non-technical                                                |
| Location           | Global                                                                       |
| Company            | Any — corporate, agency, freelance                                          |
| Tools they live in | ChatGPT free tier, Google Workspace, Excel, whatever work mandates           |
| Technical level    | Beginner. Uses AI like a search box                                          |

### Psychographics

Curious but a little intimidated. Suspects they're "using it wrong." Doesn't want to look behind the curve at work. Responds to plain talk and a clear on-ramp, not jargon and not hype.

### What they're stuck on

They re-prompt the same request four times and get junk, then blame the model. Nobody taught them how the thing actually works.

### What they want

The on-ramp. How the tools work, where to learn for free, and proof there's a better way than winging every prompt.

### Where they already are

LinkedIn, YouTube, general AI newsletters, TikTok/Reels AI explainers, Google searches like "how to use Claude" and "best AI prompts."

### Subscribe triggers

A free resource that overdelivers (the Anthropic course post). A "you're not dumb, you were never taught this" reframe. Quick wins.

### Objections to beat

"Is this over my head?" — No, this is the on-ramp. "Another AI hype account?" — No hype here.

### Landing page lines

"If you've re-prompted four times and still got junk, the problem isn't Claude." / "Treat AI like a system, not a search box."

## Who it's NOT for

Say this out loud so the page stays sharp:

- People chasing crypto hype, "AI will change everything" takes, or trend recaps.
- Pure engineers wanting deep single-topic tutorials — this lives at the marketing-meets-code seam, not in the weeds of one stack.
- Anyone who wants a daily blast. Posts land when there's something real to say.
- People looking to be told AI will do all the work for them. This is for people who want to build.

---

## The one-line ICP

A marketer or operator who's done outsourcing the technical side of growth and wants to build AI systems with their own hands — starting from wherever they are today.

Put that line, or a version of it, right above the subscribe button.

---

### A note on the numbers

The demographics and percentages are informed estimates built from your positioning, your first two posts, and where these readers actually gather — not survey data. Treat them as a starting hypothesis. Once you have 100–200 subscribers, pull the real data (Substack gives you signup source, open rates, and rough geography) and correct these profiles against it.
