# AI with Pelumi — Ideal Reader Profiles

A working reference for the landing page and every content decision after it. Three reader segments in priority order. Each one is broken down all the way: who they are, what they do, what they're stuck on, where they hang out, what makes them subscribe, and what makes them bounce.

The through-line: people who use AI at work and suspect they're using it wrong. They don't want theory or hype. They want to watch someone who actually builds walk them through the practical stack — a content engine, no-code websites and agents, an AI workforce, and prompt-to-production for real marketing assets. Pelumi is the product marketer who does this for a living and shows the wiring. The brand is the authority. The newsletter is how you stay in the room.

This replaces the old "marketer who wants to build" ICP. The audience is wider now. Still sharp, just wider.

---

## Segment 1 — The Professional Who Suspects They're Using AI Wrong (primary, ~50% of target audience)

The core reader. Everything on the landing page speaks to this person first. This used to be the top-of-funnel segment. It's the whole funnel now.

### Demographics

| Field              | Detail                                                                       |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Age                | 25–45                                                                        |
| Role               | Marketers, ops, analysts, consultants, PMs, founders, freelancers, knowledge workers |
| Seniority          | Junior to senior. The common thread is job, not level                        |
| Income             | $45k–$140k (US/UK); local and contract equivalents elsewhere                 |
| Education          | Degree-holding, usually non-technical                                        |
| Location           | Global. Strong in US, UK, Nigeria, Kenya, India, Philippines, EU             |
| Company            | Any — corporate, startup, agency, solo                                       |
| Tools they live in | ChatGPT, Claude, Google Workspace, Notion, Excel, whatever work mandates     |
| Technical level    | Uses AI like a smarter search box. Knows there's more. Doesn't know the path |

### Psychographics

Curious and a little behind, and they know it. They've watched a coworker do in ten minutes what takes them an hour, and they can't tell if it's the tool or the person. They re-prompt the same request four times and blame the model. They don't want a certificate. They want to stop feeling like the last person in the room to figure this out. They respect plain talk and someone who shows the actual steps, not screenshots of a chat with the good part cropped out.

### What they're stuck on

Nobody taught them how the tools actually work. They treat AI like autocomplete when it can run whole workflows. They've heard "you can automate that" and "you can build a skill for that" and have no idea where to start. The gap isn't intelligence. It's the on-ramp.

### What they want

The practical stack, one piece at a time. Turn one idea into a week of content. Build a website or an agent without touching code. Stand up an AI assistant that handles the busywork. Quick wins that prove there's a better way, then the confidence to go build the next thing themselves.

### Where they already are

LinkedIn, YouTube tutorials at 1.5x, TikTok and Reels AI explainers, general AI newsletters, Google and now ChatGPT searches like "how to repurpose content with AI" and "how to build a website with AI no code."

### Subscribe triggers

A free resource that overdelivers. A "you're not dumb, you were never shown this" reframe. One walkthrough they can copy today and have working by tonight.

### Objections to beat

"Is this over my head?" — No. This is the on-ramp, built for exactly where you are. "Another AI hype account?" — No hype. I show the build or I don't post.

### Landing page lines

"You're not using AI wrong. Nobody showed you the stack." / "Watch a product marketer build the AI systems, then copy them."

## Segment 2 — The Marketer / Operator Leveling Up (~35%)

The reader closest to Pelumi's own job. High conversion, high retention, most likely to become a fan and share.

### Demographics

| Field              | Detail                                                                    |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Age                | 27–40                                                                     |
| Role               | Product marketer, growth marketer, content lead, marketing manager, RevOps, founder wearing the marketing hat |
| Seniority          | 3–12 years in                                                             |
| Income             | $70k–$160k (US/UK); local and contract equivalents elsewhere              |
| Education          | Degree-holding, often non-technical                                       |
| Location           | Remote-first. US, UK, Nigeria, Kenya, India, EU                           |
| Company            | Startups and scale-ups, 10–500 people. B2B SaaS, fintech, crypto, DTC     |
| Tools they live in | Notion, HubSpot, Webflow, Zapier, Apollo, Claude/ChatGPT, Figma           |
| Technical level    | No-code power user hitting the ceiling. Reads code, can't yet write it    |

### Psychographics

Ambitious and impatient. They see AI eating the boring 60% of their job and they'd rather ride it than get replaced by it. They respect people who ship. Allergic to thought leaders who've never built anything. They want an edge that's real: the rare marketer who can run the AI systems most of their peers still outsource.

### What they're stuck on

They can spec a campaign but the technical build dies in someone's backlog. Every tool wants a subscription and still won't do the one thing they need. They've seen someone wire up in an afternoon what they'd have paid $200 a month for. It stings. It also motivates.

### What they want

To run the whole play with AI: the content engine, the no-code builds, the assistants that do outreach and research, the videos and ads. To stop being "just marketing." Systems they build once and keep.

### Where they already are

LinkedIn (heavy), X marketing and build-in-public circles, r/marketing and r/SaaS, Lenny's Newsletter, Demand Curve, indie growth Discords.

### Subscribe triggers

A workflow they can copy today. Proof the writer actually built it. A post that names the exact frustration they had this week.

### Objections to beat

"Too technical for me?" — No, that's the point, it starts where you are. "Another AI newsletter?" — No, this one shows the build.

### Landing page lines

"You know marketing. Now run it with AI." / "The systems your peers outsource, built in front of you and yours to keep."

## Segment 3 — The Solo Operator / Technical Founder (~15%)

### Demographics

| Field              | Detail                                                                    |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Age                | 25–40                                                                     |
| Role               | Indie hacker, solo founder, technical co-founder, one-person growth team  |
| Seniority          | Runs the whole thing; growth landed on them by default                    |
| Income             | Variable — pre-revenue to $200k+; measured in MRR, not salary             |
| Education          | Self-taught or CS-adjacent                                                 |
| Location           | Global, remote. Strong in US, EU, India, Nigeria, SEA                     |
| Company            | 1–5 people. Their own product                                             |
| Tools they live in | VS Code, GitHub, Vercel, Supabase, Stripe, the Claude/OpenAI APIs, Cursor |
| Technical level    | Can code. Ships. The gap is marketing systems, not engineering            |

### Psychographics

Autonomous, output-obsessed, skeptical of anything that smells like an agency deck. They read code faster than marketing copy. They'll respect you the second you show a real repo or a real pipeline. They hate fluff more than anyone.

### What they're stuck on

Growth is a tax on their time. They can build the automation but it's glue-work they keep putting off, so acquisition stays manual and slow. They don't want a course. They want the diagram.

### What they want

Marketing that runs while they build the product. Acquisition and content systems they wire once and forget.

### Where they already are

X build-in-public, Indie Hackers, Hacker News, r/SaaS, Y Combinator library, dev-heavy Discords.

### Subscribe triggers

Something they can fork. A pipeline diagram. Honest "here's what broke" writeups. Zero patience for theory.

### Objections to beat

"I can figure this out myself." — Sure, but here's the version that already works. "Is this just marketing content?" — It's the plumbing, wiring shown.

### Landing page lines

"Marketing that runs while you build the product." / "Wire it once. Get back to shipping."

## Who it's NOT for

Say this out loud so the page stays sharp:

- People chasing crypto hype, "AI changes everything" takes, or trend recaps.
- Pure engineers wanting deep single-topic tutorials. This lives at the work-meets-AI seam, not in the weeds of one stack.
- Anyone who wants a daily blast. Posts land when there's something real to say.
- People who want AI to do all the work for them. This is for people who want to learn to drive it.

## The one-line ICP

A working professional who's tired of using AI like a search box and wants to be shown the practical stack — content engines, no-code builds, AI assistants, and prompt-to-production — by someone who builds these for a living.

Put that line, or a version of it, right above the subscribe button.

---

## The topic pillars (what the authority is built on)

Every post, lead magnet, and social clip ladders up to one of four. These are also the primary search and AI-citation targets — see the PRD keyword section.

1. **Content Engine** — turn one idea into emails, blogs, newsletters, and content that keeps working for you.
2. **Build It, Don't Learn It** — create websites, AI agents, and automations without writing code.
3. **Your AI Workforce** — build AI assistants that handle outreach, meetings, social, research, and daily operations.
4. **Prompt to Production** — turn simple prompts into videos, UGC ads, presentations, flyers, and campaigns.

The umbrella above all four: *how a non-engineer uses AI to run real work.* That's the phrase the brand should own.

### A note on the numbers
